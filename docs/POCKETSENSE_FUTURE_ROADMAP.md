# PocketSense research roadmap

**Product decision memo · 21 November 2026 · Exploratory**

## Executive summary

Fund two experiments first. Measure whether a reference card improves results on a phone the team has never tested. In parallel, ask a chemistry instructor to run a colorimetry exercise with students using their own phones. The first experiment measures a dependency shared by almost every proposed feature. The second tests a real workflow using the region sampler and local exports already in the app. Both can fail cheaply, without collecting personal images.

A paint swatch shortlist merits a small consumer trial if a catalog owner permits offline use of measured swatch data. Multiple-pad interpretation merits a research partnership if a lab supplies a defined device, controls and an independent comparator. Skin color documentation could serve longitudinal studies, but a change smaller than capture variability has little value. Defer lesion measurements and clinical test interpretation until partners can validate the intended use and study how users interpret the output.

The team reports that its UC San Diego CES Hackathon award and the 21 November CES × CAST-SD presentation prompted these ideas. Those conversations identify people to interview. Demand, willingness to supply reference hardware, purchasing authority and willingness to pay remain **open questions**. No market share or revenue estimate underlies this ordering.

## Starting point and evidence

The official repository at `d3732a0` decodes images to browser sRGB, linearizes the channels and computes D65 CIELAB. A user can select a rectangle, inspect channel medians and extract a palette. The pH engine projects a sample onto a Catmull–Rom curve through labeled reference colors using Euclidean Lab distance. Its 0.01 pH search spacing describes numerical resolution. Establishing measurement accuracy requires independent specimens and a reference instrument. Calibration between cameras, alignment across visits and multi-zone assay interpretation would each require new validation. [Scientific methods](SCIENCE.md) · [Verification scope](VALIDATION.md)

**Evidence** below describes a published experiment, an existing product, an official policy or the inspected implementation. **Hypotheses** are proposed extensions with a test that could disprove their value. **Speculation** includes commercial adoption, performance on devices outside the study and progression to clinical products. The matrix and research order are team-facing judgments. The cited experiments used specific devices, materials and lighting.

### A. Color calibration

**Evidence.** Nixon and colleagues combined device-specific calibration with flash/no-flash image pairs to improve chromaticity measurements under changing ambient illumination. Their capture conditions differ from a browser receiving an arbitrary processed JPEG. Finlayson and colleagues showed why higher-order polynomial terms can behave poorly as exposure changes, and developed root-polynomial correction with predictable exposure scaling for linear responses. [Smartphone experiments](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0230561) · [Root-polynomial correction](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIP.2015.2405336)

**Hypothesis.** A researcher who photographs a patch on two phones will obtain more reproducible Lab values if they include a characterized card in the same light and plane. Start with diagonal gains from an unsaturated neutral patch. This handles a simple color cast. A neutral alone cannot characterize channel mixing. Fit a regularized 3×3 linear RGB-to-XYZ matrix from several independent colors. Compare ordinary polynomial and root-polynomial fits when residuals show a systematic pattern. Convert corrected XYZ to Lab for evaluation against reference values under the same illuminant, observer and white point. A card specified under D50 needs a documented common reference before scoring against D65. Lab conversion alone provides no camera calibration.

Add correction as an optional transformation of sampled colors. Retain original RGB, target ID, profile version and residuals in local outputs. Auto white balance, local tone mapping, clipping and spatially mixed illumination can defeat a global transform. Test glare, shadows, faded cards and near-saturated channels. Permit a retake when too few usable reference patches remain. Benchmark a separately captured chart against same-frame results before promising portability across sessions. Use volunteered chart photos and cut away surrounding personal content. This pilot addresses nonclinical color consistency.

### B. Skin color documentation

**Evidence.** A study of physical skin-tone standards found camera angle changed reported color even on flat swatches. It provides a capture warning and a reproducible bench model. Performance on skin also depends on site, curvature and physiology. [Camera distance and angle study](https://doi.org/10.1002/jbio.202200381)

**Hypothesis.** A study coordinator could document relative color at a defined site between visits using a same-plane external card, consistent distance and pose, and a manually confirmed region. Show signed ΔL*, Δa*, Δb* alongside ΔE and repeatability from repeated captures. A nearby reference area on the same participant may absorb some common lighting variation, but compare both regions separately. The reference area can also change with pressure, blood flow or sun exposure. A relative difference could hide a real common change. Begin with a landmark overlay and manually repositioned regions. Consider local image registration only after measuring operator error.

Measure within-session repeats, then between-day recaptures with repositioning, stratified by tone, body site, device and lighting. Compare agreement with independent colorimeter readings using matched sites and documented contact conditions. A cohort correlation can look high while within-person changes remain unreliable. Withhold quantitative change if its scale is comparable to the repeatability limit in the intended population. Recruit through a research partner with consent and institutional review as applicable. Provide opt-in local retention, crop, export and deletion. Treat skin photos and dates as potentially identifying. The proposed use is color documentation. Clinical interpretation would require a new intended-use assessment.

### C. Lesion and mole photographs

**Evidence.** Huang and colleagues studied correspondence between lesions across 3D total-body scans using landmarks and texture. This shows that identifying the same feature is itself a measurement problem. Transferring its results to two casually posed 2D phone photographs would require a separate study. [Serial image correspondence study](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~misha/MyPapers/MICCAI23.pdf)

**Hypothesis.** A research team may benefit from manually paired, locally viewed photographs with an alignment overlay, annotated boundaries and color distributions. Require user confirmation of the same site, visible landmarks and a same-plane scale. Analyze area only when spatial scale and perspective support it. Measure shape in the original calibrated image. A flexible registration that warps the lesion itself could erase exactly the change being studied. Later segmentation proposals should expose editable outlines and their variation between operators.

Start with fixed printed or synthetic shapes under pose changes. Measure landmark error, area error and false apparent change in unchanged targets before considering consented paired images. Test repeat traces by independent annotators, occlusion and incorrect pairings. Do not build a consumer alert, disease classifier or risk score. A partner must establish that participants understand what an overlay shows, define withdrawal and deletion, and review intended use with qualified advisers before any human pilot. Derive no clinical decision from lack of visible change. This higher-cost extension stays off the near-term build list. [FDA device software guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/policy-device-software-functions-and-mobile-medical-applications)

### D. Wall color finder

**Evidence.** Benjamin Moore combines a phone-camera capture workflow with catalog browsing and separate Bluetooth color readers. It encourages testing a physical sample in the room. That establishes a competitor and a plausible interaction, while leaving independent accuracy and demand for PocketSense unresolved. Physical reflectance varies by wavelength. Two finishes can have similar tristimulus values under one light and diverge under another (metamerism). A single RGB photo does not uniquely determine those reflectance spectra. [Color Portfolio product](https://www.benjaminmoore.com/en-us/color-portfolio-paint-matching-app) · [X-Rite color measurement guide](https://www.xrite.com/-/media/xrite/files/literature/l11/l11-000_l11-099/l11-029_color_guide_and_glossary/l11-029_color_guide_en.pdf)

**Hypothesis.** A renter choosing sample pots wants a short list of chips to compare at home. Sample several matte wall regions with an optional card. Rank licensed physical chip measurements at a declared illuminant using ΔE00. Call the results approximate, show alternative chips, and ask the user to compare actual paint under relevant room lights. Display HEX and screen previews inherit camera and monitor rendering. Gloss, texture, shadows and substrate can shift ranks. A spectrophotometer with controlled illumination and measurement geometry can collect spectral data useful for professional formulation or multi-light evaluation.

Benchmark top-three retrieval on blinded physical chips and aged wall panels, including close neutrals, with independent instrument readings. Compare against uncorrected image matching and the user's normal fan deck. Measure time to select physical samples and whether the user accepts one. A useful result can reduce shopping time even when the top chip changes with the lamp. Without redistributable catalog data and observed user benefit, stop at local palette export. A licensed offline catalog and local cropped wall photos fit the architecture. Machine learning cannot reconstruct a unique spectrum from one RGB triplet.

### E. Assay-specific point-of-care research

**Evidence.** Smith and colleagues tested a mobile dipstick reader with controlled lighting, sample delivery and read timing. Reference-assisted LFA imaging has also been demonstrated with a geometric and color card. These results depend on each assay and its capture protocol. [Dipstick experiment](https://doi.org/10.1039/C6LC00340K) · [Lateral-flow normalization study](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.2c00793)

**Hypothesis.** A lab developing one colorimetric test may want an auditable per-zone optical signal while it optimizes the test. Start with one partner's research format and nonpatient materials. Represent its layout, polarity, color channel, timing window, expected controls and calibration curve as a versioned protocol. Subtract local paper background. A broad dipstick pad may use a robust central region, while a lateral-flow line may need integrated contrast against adjacent membrane. A test/control ratio is reasonable only where the control response supports that normalization. Preserve raw intensities and control outcomes. A failed control, saturation or missed time window should yield an invalid run.

Validation needs independent known concentrations, blanks, low/high controls, reference-method agreement, repeatability and range limits. Challenge lot, operator, timing, colored/turbid sample interference and phone changes. Fit curves separately when lot evidence requires it. Record invalid rates as well as error on accepted runs. An ML segmenter needs labels for faint lines and difficult negatives, plus manual correction. The existing local region sampler is reusable. The pH spline describes a different response and cannot stand in for an assay standard curve. Commercial relevance depends on obtaining a partner with permission to use its consumables and comparative data. Keep assay data local. For patient-facing use, review instrument, software and test together. In the US, actual intended use and claims matter even with a research-use label. [FDA research-use IVD guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/distribution-in-vitro-diagnostic-products-labeled-research-use-only-or-investigational-use-only)

### F. Shared-reservoir paper and microfluidic devices

**Evidence.** Patterned paper has directed a single sample through channels into separate test regions and a control. It establishes the feasibility of the geometry. Independent delivery, reproducibility and calibration for a new cartridge still need experiments. [Original multiplexed paper-device study](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.200603817)

**Hypothesis.** A device developer could compare multiple pad signals and replicate runs in one local view. Use a marked device template, manually confirm orientation, and reuse the region sampler for each pad and its nearby background. Provide separate elapsed times, units, reference normalization and invalid reasons per pad. Avoid a combined score across analytes. Scaled overlays, a run identifier and local CSV export could replace error-prone transcription.

Test image registration independently from transport. In a nonclinical bench experiment use inert colored standards to challenge partial wetting, unequal arrival, cross-talk, evaporation, paper variability and uneven light. Verify pad-to-pad identity, time alignment and signal variance across fabrication batches against an independent reader. Multiple zones from the same cartridge are correlated observations. Hold out entire cartridges and lots. Machine segmentation is optional if fiducials and a rigid template suffice. A reliable partner-supplied cartridge and manufacturing controls are prerequisites. Commercial prospects remain speculative without them. Keep partner layouts and results local. A later medical intended use would add the assay-specific obligations above.

### G. Classroom colorimetry and lab notebooks

**Evidence.** Kehoe and Penn demonstrated classroom concentration measurements with camera phones and a controlled arrangement of blanks, standards and samples in one frame. This offers a practical first lesson using inexpensive, nonclinical material. [Camera-phone teaching experiment](https://doi.org/10.1021/ed300567p)

**Hypothesis.** Students could compare a food-dye dilution series, choose a channel and graph relative signal against known concentration. A teacher could use the same exercise to show how changing the light or clipping a channel invalidates a fit. Begin with the existing sampler and exported values in a worksheet. Fix optical path and capture geometry. In transmission, a blank-referenced intensity may support an absorbance estimate within a tested range. Reflectance from paper has a different physical interpretation. Favor manual selection and a transparent curve over a learned predictor.

Ask the instructor to compare time spent transcribing results, independent student completion and interpretation of a blinded unknown against the existing lesson. Store group labels locally and avoid collecting names or classroom pictures. The pilot targets teaching, with no regulated measurement claim. Test willingness to run the lesson a second time before adding notebook infrastructure. Environmental water assays or materials-aging studies could later reuse a successful worksheet, but each would need its own standards, comparator and users before receiving a dedicated tool.

## Machine learning feasibility

| Decision | Deterministic reference correction | Hybrid calibration or segmentation | End-to-end image inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Benefit to test | Remove neutral casts and reproducible channel bias, expose residuals | Fit residual nonlinearity or save operators time locating patches | Learn complex image-to-color, illumination or assay relationships within a defined domain |
| Data needed | Characterized patch values with specified white point and repeated independent captures | Baseline data plus device/session diversity, labeled regions if segmenting, independent target readings | Broad independently labeled images spanning devices, operators, material or subject variation, illuminants and assay lots |
| Main failure | Clipped data, nonuniform local processing, chart undercoverage | Overfit chart families or device identities, unstable extrapolation | Learn backgrounds, assay packaging or acquisition sites as shortcuts, shift after OS updates |
| Generalization test | Unseen reference patches and at least one unseen phone/light combination | Same splits, ablate each learned step, include unsupported devices | Locked external dataset from unseen sites/devices. For human studies split by participant, for assays by specimen and lot |
| Compute and offline fit | Region statistics and a small matrix fit on-device in ordinary JS | Small coefficients or an optimized local model. Test bundle size, latency and peak memory | Compact quantized inference may fit browser runtimes. Check cold-start, memory, thermal behavior and offline asset caching on older phones |
| Interpretation | Inspect gains, coefficients, residuals and input gamut | Retain manual overrides, reference residuals and the baseline output | Outputs harder to audit. Needs calibrated failure detection and explicit unsupported conditions |
| Maintenance | Version card measurements and transforms, retest browser decoding | Version model, training data and supported device families, rerun holdouts | Ongoing consent, dataset stewardship, model/runtime updates and external revalidation |
| Recommendation | First experiment | Try only for a demonstrated residual error or annotation bottleneck | Defer until several independent cohorts and a material unmet performance target justify the cost |

Root-polynomial regression can serve as a deterministic fitted baseline. Here “hybrid” means adding a learned component to an explicit reference-based workflow. In one camera correction benchmark, a neural network improved on simple linear least squares but fell behind root-polynomial regression. Exposure changes also challenged the fixed-exposure network. The authors' sensor data and comparisons motivate a benchmark in PocketSense's processed-image pipeline. They do not predict which method will win there. [Kucuk et al., primary comparative study](https://doi.org/10.3390/jimaging9100214)

## Experiments and decision gates

1. **Establish the decision before collecting data.** Interview a lab operator, teaching instructor and paint user about the last real task, available reference materials, acceptable error, cost of a wrong result, setup time and existing alternative. Recruit beyond conference attendees. Use voluntary feedback categories to find participants who opted into contact. A feature-request count indicates interest among submitters and supplies no denominator for adoption.
2. **Benchmark uncorrected, neutral-only and card-matrix results.** Counterbalance phones, lights and operators. Capture repeats on different days and compare independently characterized chips to each prediction at one declared white point. Reserve different physical targets for scoring. Hold out devices, illuminants and sessions together. Report paired median and 95th-percentile ΔE00, signed component bias, between-phone reproducibility, retake rate and setup time. Estimate uncertainty by resampling independent sessions or devices, not pixels. Advance if the held-out improvement exceeds a pre-agreed task tolerance, including worst-condition and usability criteria. If the chart only helps on fitted patches, narrow supported conditions or stop.
3. **Try a lesson and a chip-selection task.** Run the nonclinical classroom exercise against the instructor's usual procedure. Offer a paint trial only after catalog permissions and appropriate measurements are available. Continue each direction independently if users complete the actual task more reliably or quickly and ask to repeat it. Stop if card handling costs more time than it saves or the shortlist performs like uncorrected sampling.
4. **Pilot one partner protocol.** A lab must supply versioned controls, batches and an independent comparator before work begins on a strip or multiplex reader. Freeze the analysis rules before blinded comparisons. Use held-out batches to test recovery of known levels, repeatability, invalid calls and time-window sensitivity. For skin research instead, require consent, matched site measurements, representative recruitment and a repeatability limit smaller than the smallest change the partner needs to document. Reconsider any path that only works on its developers' devices.
5. **Escalate complexity only on evidence.** Add a small regression or segmentation model if it solves a named failure and beats the best deterministic comparator by a pre-agreed margin on untouched data. Report errors and exclusions by phone and relevant material or participant subgroup. Test offline memory and latency against a budget measured on the oldest supported phone. Revisit end-to-end learning only if a simple method still misses the user's decision threshold and the partner can sustain external validation. Defer public quantitative lesion tools until correspondence, measurement and user interpretation pass separate studies.

The near-term release can invite feedback on these experiments while existing tools remain directly available. Decide on the next build after reviewing paired measurement data and observed user tasks. New evidence about card burden, catalog rights, underserved users or the availability of testing partners can change the order.
